# Writing-for-the-web research briefs (2026-07-02)

Inputs behind mindpattern-v3 `docs/specs/site-writer-rules.md` (the spec the
site writer and critic agents are prompted with). Two research passes:
evidence/UX and editorial craft. Key findings with sources.

## Evidence side (usability + readability research)

- **NN/g, How Users Read on the Web**: 79% scan, 16% read word-by-word.
  Concise (-50% words) +58% usability; scannable layout +47%; objective
  language +27%; combined +124%.
- **NN/g, How Little Do Users Read** (n=45,237 page views): ~20-28% of words
  read per visit; 50% of text read only on pages ≤111 words.
- **NN/g, F-Shaped Pattern** (2006 + 2017): F-scanning is a symptom of
  unformatted text; countered by front-loaded first two paragraphs,
  information-rich headings, bullets.
- **NN/g, First 2 Words**: scanners see ~11 characters of a headline/link;
  front-load the entity/finding.
- **NN/g, World's Best Headlines (BBC)**: 5 words / 34 chars average; short,
  information-rich, front-loaded, zero-context, predictable.
- **NN/g Microcontent**: titles 40-60 chars, keywords first, no puns; must
  work out of context.
- **NN/g Plain Language for Experts**: experts prefer plain text; grade 10-12
  ceiling even for expert audiences; sentences 15-20 words; paragraphs 1-2
  sentences; plain language raises perceived credibility.
- **American Press Institute**: comprehension ~100% at 8-word sentences, 90%
  at 14, <10% at 43. Average 14-20 words, hard cap 25 (also GOV.UK's line).
- **GOV.UK / Sarah Winters Content Design**: user-need first; summaries ≤160
  chars; front-load titles with searched words; active voice; no FAQs;
  descriptive links (also WCAG).
- **Clickbait research** (CHI 2021; Sci. Reports 2024): readers prefer and
  click non-clickbait; detected manipulation suppresses trust and sharing;
  optimal headline = specific facts, not a tease.
- **SEO limits**: title tag 50-60 chars; meta description ~120-158 chars
  (mobile ~120).
- **Google helpful content / E-E-A-T**: rewards original analysis, first-hand
  experience, descriptive non-sensational headings, clear who/how/why;
  penalizes scaled summarizing-without-value. AI-slop tells readers punish:
  stock abstractions, "not just X, it's Y", em-dash overuse, hedged
  non-conclusions, uniform rhythm.

## Editorial craft side (newsroom + analyst practice)

- **Axios Smart Brevity**: muscular headline ≤6 words, strongest word first;
  one-sentence lede = the single most interesting thing; bolded "Why it
  matters" axiom right after; depth is opt-in ("Go deeper"); one idea per
  sentence; ~40% shorter than conventional prose. "Short, not shallow."
- **Economist style guide**: clarity of writing follows clarity of thought;
  Orwell's six rules; "do not be stuffy"; no didactic openers (Consider,
  Note, Imagine reads like a textbook).
- **AP lede rules**: one sentence ≤30 words; main active verb inside the
  first 7 words; attribution at the END of the lede.
- **Reuters Handbook**: named source beats unnamed; be explicit about what
  you don't know; never opinion in news copy, label analysis.
- **Bloomberg Way**: headline in 63 chars written before the story; nut graf
  says what's at stake.
- **Stratechery**: open with the news, reframe through a named recurring
  framework; authority = consistency over time; the archive is the credential.
- **Money Stuff (Levine)**: naive first-principles setup, then the news;
  epistemic humility as voice; hedges live in footnotes so the text stays
  punchy.
- **The Information**: every piece contains something you won't find
  elsewhere; briefing items = link + judgment, never summary.
- **Ann Handley**: ugly first draft then rewrite; utility x inspiration x
  empathy; most important words at sentence start; "would you say it to your
  sweetheart in the dark?"
- **Dek craft (Verge/Wired/Atlantic practice)**: hed = the what, dek = the
  so-what; the dek must add new information, never restate.
- **Opinion craft (OpEd Project / WaPo)**: evidence before opinion; stake a
  falsifiable claim; concede the strongest counterargument once; ban
  "time will tell".

Ten slop separators distilled: verb in first 7 words; one idea per sentence;
lede is chosen not summarized; specific terminal attribution; numbers over
adjectives; say what you don't know; no didactic imperatives or qualifier
pile-ups; furniture advances, never echoes; falsifiable singular take; end
when the information ends.
